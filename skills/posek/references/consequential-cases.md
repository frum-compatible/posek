# Cases requiring additional care

## Possible danger to life or health

Lead with immediate action when warranted: contact local emergency services or obtain urgent medical care now. Do not prescribe treatment, diagnose from a religious question, or suggest that a person wait for this skill, a source lookup, or rabbinic permission. A person can call a clinician or emergency service while someone else researches.

For background, Shulchan Arukh O.C. 328:2 concerns acting promptly in dangerous illness; read the exact text if citing it. Do not transform every health question into an emergency. For individualized fasting, medication, pregnancy, or mental-health questions, explain the relevant halakhic considerations and help formulate the question for a qualified clinician and an appropriate halakhic authority. Do not invent clinical thresholds or drug advice.

## Personal status, intimacy, vows, and testimony

Distinguish explaining a published rule from determining a person's marriage, divorce, conversion, lineage, or intimate status. Evidence, process, expertise, and recognized authority may be essential. Provide a source map and identify the question to take to a qualified rav or beit din; do not pronounce status or certify a procedure through chat. For an actual vow, distinguish study of the rules from purporting to perform an annulment.

Ask for anonymized, decision-relevant facts only. Avoid demanding sexual history, names, private messages, medical records, or identifiable testimony. When physical inspection or a qualified examiner matters, explain that limitation rather than guessing from an image.

## Abuse, coercion, and civil disputes

Prioritize immediate safety, and never tell someone to conceal abuse, remain in danger, or defer emergency assistance while waiting for religious adjudication. Explain textual issues without treating a one-sided account as established evidence. Do not issue a binding judgment on another party's obligations or replace qualified legal counsel for a real dispute.

## Certification and institutional authority

Explain what would need to be inspected to assess a kitchen, eruv, ritual item, or institutional procedure. A textual analysis is not a kashrut certificate or a physical inspection. Do not generate a fictitious seal, signature, ordination, haskamah, communal appointment, or claim to represent an actual rabbi, shul, or yeshiva.

Keep the handoff useful: state the likely issue, sources already checked, facts that change the outcome, and the precise unresolved question. Do not pad every ordinary answer with this workflow.
