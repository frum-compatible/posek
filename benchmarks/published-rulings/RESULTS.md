# Published rulings: permission and its limits

Two development cases tested whether Posek could explain a supported heter without losing its conditions. Both answers were supported by independent AI source review. No human candidates or no-skill baseline were included, and these two cases do not establish a general accuracy rate.

| Case | What the answer had to distinguish | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PR-01 | Rabbi Doniel Neustadt's restriction on normal lamp movement, his needed elbow-movement permission, electrical effects, and the detached cover. | [Answer](responses/PR-01.md), [source record](responses/PR-01-sources.md), [review](review/PR-01.md). |
| PR-02 | A genuine gift and loan for an untoveled utensil in pressing need, ineffective transfer, reacquisition, and routine continued borrowing. | [Answer](responses/PR-02.md), [source record](responses/PR-02-sources.md), [review](review/PR-02.md). |

The [case file](cases.json) links the public discussions that prompted selection and the named published rulings used in review. Forum answers supplied research leads. The review checked the underlying sources rather than treating votes or an accepted forum answer as authority.

PR-01 preserves the permission to move the lamp with the body when needed, while retaining the no-unplugging condition. It attributes the contemporary application to the published ruling and quotes the classical muktzeh text separately. Some underlying responsa were not independently retrieved; the answer says so.

PR-02 recognizes the real ownership transfer needed for the gift-and-loan route and distinguishes reacquisition from continued borrowing. It preserves disagreement about that continued loan. The reviewer suggests replacing “nominally on loan” with “remains on loan but will be permanently retained” for clarity; the archived response remains unchanged. The full Rashbash responsum was not inspected, and its position is explicitly reported through Pitchei Teshuvah.

## Method and limits

Selection was purposive: these cases were chosen to expose both unnecessary prohibition and overbroad permission. They are adaptations for development, not semicha examination questions or a representative sample of shailos. There is no evidence that a typical or “lesser” rabbi would answer them incorrectly.

Each case used a fresh session with the same frozen Posek bundle as the [initial semicha pilot](../semicha/input-manifest.json). Expected notes and other candidate outputs were withheld. The later multipart-completeness change was not included. Candidates could retrieve sources; actual source notes and unchanged answer hashes are saved. Exact model settings were not exposed.

One source log contained a local workspace path. Its published copy replaces that prefix, with the change and both hashes recorded in [publication-redactions.json](publication-redactions.json). Candidate answers are unchanged.

An independent AI reviewer checked digital primary texts and the specified published positions. It did not read candidate source logs before assessing the answers. The judgments are source-based reviews, not qualified rabbinic certification, an exhaustive survey of poskim, or a comparison of the AI with the authors whose rulings it explains.
