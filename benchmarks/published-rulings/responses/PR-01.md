L’maaseh, following Rabbi Doniel Neustadt’s published approach: **moving the whole lit lamp normally by hand is assur under the stringent practice he follows; nudging it with an elbow when needed to sleep is mutar**, on your assumptions. His article explicitly addresses this sleep-disturbance case. [Rabbi Neustadt, “Moving an Electric Lamp on Shabbos”](https://outorah.org/p/49935/)

The source distinction is between the object’s muktzeh status and the manner of moving it. The Mechaber rules that a lamp lit for that Shabbos remains forbidden to move even after extinguishing, and that ordinary movement is forbidden even for use of the lamp or its place. These are the classical lamp rules; applying them to an electric lamp requires the later authorities, not merely an assumption that the Shulchan Aruch discussed electricity. [Shulchan Arukh, Orach Chayim 279:1–2](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Orach_Chayim.279.1)

Separately, the Mechaber states: **“וטלטול בגופו אפי' לצורך דבר האסור מותר”**—my translation: “Moving with one’s body is permitted even for the sake of the prohibited object.” Mishnah Berurah 311:30 explains that this means movement without the hand, using the body or other limbs, and treats it more leniently than ordinary indirect movement. This is a muktzeh permission; the object need not cease being muktzeh. [Shulchan Arukh, Orach Chayim 311:8](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Orach_Chayim.311.8); [Mishnah Berurah 311:30](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Berurah.311.30)

**No, this permission does not cover pulling out the plug:** Rabbi Neustadt explicitly excludes that result. The muktzeh permission does not authorize disconnecting and extinguishing the light. His separate permission to rotate a Kosher Lamp’s completely detached, non-muktzeh cylinder applies to that cover, not the electrical lamp body or an arbitrary attached lampshade. [Rabbi Neustadt, paragraphs 2–3](https://outorah.org/p/49935/)

He records disagreement over the lamp’s classification, citing Igros Moshe for treating it as a kli shemelachto le’issur, but follows stringent practice for ordinary movement. I verified his article and the classical passages above; I did not independently inspect every cited responsum.
