**L’maaseh: yes, a genuine gift followed by borrowing is mutar for the Shabbos meal described.** The Shulchan Aruch addresses precisely someone who forgot to immerse before Shabbos: “יתננו לעובד כוכבים במתנה ואחר כך ישאלנו ממנו ומותר להשתמש בו” — give it to the non-Jew, then borrow it and use it (my translation). This is the Mechaber’s ruling in **Yoreh De’ah 120:16**, also stated in **Orach Chayim 323:7**. Its basis is that a utensil borrowed from a non-Jew does not require immersion, **YD 120:8**. [Shulchan Aruch, YD 120](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_קכ); [OC 323:7](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_אורח_חיים_שכג).

The friend’s objection about gifts overlooks the exception for a Shabbos need. **Mishnah Berurah 323:34** explains: “הכא שרי משום צורך שבת” — here it is permitted because it serves Shabbos. This is permission for the transfer needed to use this utensil, not blanket permission for gifts. The premise that immersion itself is unanimously forbidden is also inaccurate: the Mechaber records both permission and prohibition, then recommends the gift-and-borrow solution to satisfy both. **Mishnah Berurah 323:30–33** explains the concern that immersion resembles making a utensil fit for use, and reports the Rema’s ruling in Darkei Moshe to follow the prohibiting position. The proposed gift avoids needing to resolve that dispute. [Mishnah Berurah, OC 323](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/משנה_ברורה_על_אורח_חיים_שכג).

**Ownership must actually pass.** The non-Jew must become the owner through an effective gift, and the Jew must thereafter hold the utensil as a borrower with the owner’s permission. Merely calling it a gift while retaining ownership does nothing: the utensil remains the Jew’s unimmersed utensil. Giving possession temporarily is not itself giving ownership. This follows from the ownership distinction in **YD 120:8,16**. There is also a material objection to the device itself: **Pitchei Teshuvah, YD 120:15**, reports that **Responsa Rashbash 468** questions it as impermissible *ha’aramah*, a contrived transaction. I verified that report in Pitchei Teshuvah; I could not retrieve the full responsum. It should therefore not be presented as a universally uncontested arrangement, although the Shulchan Aruch expressly permits it. [Pitchei Teshuvah 120:15, retrieved Hebrew text](https://www.sefaria.org/api/v3/texts/Pitchei%20Teshuva%20on%20Shulchan%20Arukh%2C%20Yoreh%20De%27ah%20120%3A15?version=source&version=english&fill_in_missing_segments=0&return_format=default).

**After Shabbos, distinguish two cases:**

- **The Jew actually reacquires ownership**, whether by purchase or a genuine gift back: the ordinary immersion obligation applies before further food use. For the definitely obligated metal food utensil described, immerse with the usual berachah. **YD 120:11** requires immersion when a Jew sells a utensil to a non-Jew and reacquires it; **120:3** gives the berachah. Rav Eliezer Melamed explicitly recommends receiving this gifted utensil back after Shabbos and immersing it with a berachah. [Shulchan Aruch, YD 120:3,11](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_קכ); [Peninei Halakhah, Kashrut 31:7](https://ph.yhb.org.il/17-31-07/).
- **It remains nominally on loan and will stay permanently with the Jew:** **Taz, YD 120:18**, treats this solution as temporary, for Shabbos or until a mikveh becomes available. Because the utensil will remain with the Jew, he requires immersion afterward; owing to uncertainty about the berachah, he advises including another definitely obligated utensil. **Mishnah Berurah 323:35** rules to immerse without a berachah, or together with another utensil whose berachah covers both. This ruling does not require an express repurchase first. [Taz 120:18, retrieved Hebrew text](https://www.sefaria.org/api/v3/texts/Turei%20Zahav%20on%20Shulchan%20Arukh%2C%20Yoreh%20De%27ah%20120%3A18?version=source&version=english&fill_in_missing_segments=0&return_format=default); [Mishnah Berurah 323:35](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/משנה_ברורה_על_אורח_חיים_שכג).

**Do not adopt this as the routine substitute for immersion when a mikveh is available.** The Rema’s weekday extension in **YD 120:16** specifies a place without a mikveh. **Aruch HaShulchan, YD 120:60**, explicitly limits the dispensation until after Shabbos or until a mikveh is found, then requires immersion without a berachah. That provides a clear practical course for this case. [Aruch HaShulchan 120:60, retrieved Hebrew text](https://www.sefaria.org/api/v3/texts/Arukh%20HaShulchan%2C%20Yoreh%20De%27ah%20120%3A60?version=source&version=english&fill_in_missing_segments=0&return_format=default).

There is a more lenient position on continued borrowing: **Rav Eliezer Melamed, Peninei Halakhah, Kashrut 31:7 and note 10**, rules that if the Jew expressly intends never to reacquire the utensil, it remains non-Jewish property and may continue to be used without immersion. He nevertheless recommends reacquiring it to fulfill the mitzvah. Thus a genuinely continuing non-Jewish ownership arrangement is disputed; an ineffective “gift” is no solution at all. The straightforward course here is a real gift and loan for Shabbos, followed by genuine reacquisition and immersion with a berachah. [Peninei Halakhah 31:7](https://ph.yhb.org.il/17-31-07/).
