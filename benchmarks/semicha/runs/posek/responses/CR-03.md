**(a) Why may pieces be salted one above another?**

The starting sugya is **Chullin 113a**, Rav Sheshet’s practice of salting each piece separately and the Gemara’s conclusion, *ella lo shena*: the concern about one piece absorbing another’s blood does not prohibit ordinary salting together. Three explanations must be distinguished:

1. **Rashi: active expulsion prevents absorption — איידי דטריד למיפלט לא בלע.** Rashi, **113a, s.v. אלא לא שנא**, explains that while the pieces expel blood they do not absorb, and when one finishes, the other has finished. Tosafot challenges the apparent synchronization: pieces are added successively, so the bottom one can finish first. The developed “no absorption” answer is that it continues expelling **ציר**, meat juice, after its blood has finished. This continued activity protects it from the upper piece’s blood. The Mechaber adopts that formulation in **YD 70:1**: “וכל זמן שפולטת צירה אינה בולעת” — while it expels its juice, it does not absorb blood. [Rashi, Chullin 113a](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חולין_קיג_א); [Shulchan Arukh YD 70:1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_ע_א).

2. **Rabbeinu Tam: דם משרק שריק — the blood runs off.** With proper drainage, blood from the upper piece slides away rather than becoming absorbed in the lower one. Thus the heter does not depend on the lower piece still releasing its own blood or juice. Beit Yosef reports Rabbeinu Tam permitting fresh salting over meat salted even several days previously; he also records the opposition to this leniency. **Source:** Beit Yosef, **YD 70**, in the discussion beginning *כתב הרשב״א אסור למלוח בשר עם בשר שכבר נמלח*, quoting the Ran, and the following paragraph. This attribution was verified in Beit Yosef’s quotation, not independently in Rabbeinu Tam’s own work. [Beit Yosef, YD 70](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/טור_יורה_דעה_ע#בית_יוסף).

3. **R. Yosef of Orléans, in Tosafot: uninterrupted absorption and expulsion — כבולעו כך פולטו.** If the lower piece was still expelling when blood from the upper piece began entering it, its channels remain open: it expels what it absorbs. Successive pieces may maintain that continuity. Unlike Rabbeinu Tam’s explanation, this requires that the lower piece has not already rested from expelling. Tosafot also gives an anonymous related explanation: ongoing expulsion of the lower piece’s juice carries out the blood it absorbs. Neither formulation says that no absorption occurred. **Source:** Tosafot, **Chullin 112b, s.v. ודגים רפו קרמייהו ופלטי ועופות קמיטי**. [Tosafot, Chullin 112b](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חולין_קיב_ב#תוספות).

The principal **nafka minos** are:

- **Removing the lower piece:** on “no absorption,” its own completed salting suffices; on the two “absorbs and expels” formulations, Tosafot requires waiting until the upper piece completes its requisite period. Bach, **YD 70, opening paragraph**, expressly identifies this difference and explains the Tur’s choice of the no-absorption formulation. [Bach, YD 70](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/טור_יורה_דעה_ע#בית_חדש).
- **A lower piece that has finished all expulsion:** Rabbeinu Tam’s runoff explanation can still permit; the active-expulsion and continuity explanations cannot simply do so.
- **Hollows containing liquid:** simple runoff alone leaves a difficulty, explicitly raised by Tosafot. The juice-expulsion explanations address it. The Mechaber permits ordinary hollows between pieces; the Rema nevertheless requires turning a concave piece downward **l’chatchilah**, while permitting **b’dieved** (70:1). This does not license salting in a vessel without drainage.

**(b) External cold blood falling onto meat during salting**

Assume actual separate liquid blood, **דם בעין**, falls onto moist meat during effective kashering salting. This differs from blood emerging from another piece in the normal salting process.

The **Rema, YD 70:6**, expressly rules that the meat is prohibited. His reason is that, for דם בעין, neither active expulsion preventing absorption nor subsequent expulsion supplies the usual heter: “לא אמרינן איידי דטריד לפלוט לא בלע, ולא כבולעו כך פולטו.” One cannot simply continue salting and assume that the foreign blood will leave. [Rema, YD 70:6](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_ע_ו).

Its being **cold** does not rescue it. **Taz 70:2** explains that the meat itself is halakhically hot through salting, comparable to a roast receiving blood. Moreover, the rule *tahor maliach vetamei tafel*—salted permitted food alongside unsalted forbidden food—does not protect moist salted meat from a **liquid** issur: the Rema explicitly distinguishes liquid from a dry forbidden solid in **91:5**. Thus an explanation relying only on “the blood was unsalted” is insufficient. [Taz 70:2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_ע_א#ט"ז_-_טורי_זהב); [Rema, YD 91:5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_צא_ה).

The ordinary measures also differ: **the Mechaber’s general rule for non-spreading issur absorbed through salting is כדי קליפה**, removal of a surface layer; **the Rema’s ordinary practice is to assess sixty**, rather than rely on that limited penetration. See **105:9** and Shakh **76:5**, which expressly includes blood in the Ashkenazic salting practice. The specific sentence about external blood during salting is the **Rema’s gloss in 70:6**, not a quotation from the Mechaber. [YD 105:9](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_קה_ט); [Shakh 76:5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_עו_א#ש"ך_-_שפתי_כהן).

Nor should “during salting” be turned into an automatic permission immediately afterward: **Shakh 70:44** points to **91:5**, where relying on the salt’s having lost its heat afterward is restricted to the stated exceptional circumstances.

**(c) External cold blood dripping onto a roast**

The **Mechaber, YD 76:1**, distinguishes the roast’s own blood from foreign blood. Fire draws out its own blood, but if other blood drips onto it, even cold blood, “לא אמרינן שהאש שואבו”; it prohibits **כדי נטילה**, a finger-thick removal at the affected place. This is deeper than the thin *kelipah* associated with ordinary salting. [Shulchan Arukh YD 76:1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_עו_א).

**Shakh 76:4** gives two explanations for why cold blood is absorbed: **תתאה גבר**, the hot lower roast heats the cold blood above, or, because the roast remains on the fire, the contact is treated as hot entering hot. **Shakh 76:5** explains that Ashkenazic practice requires **sixty against the blood**, following the Rema’s rules for salting and roasting. The Rema’s lack of a gloss at 76:1 does not signify adoption of נטילה alone.

**Taz 76:1** makes the essential scope distinction: “other blood” here means **דם בעין**, excluding ordinary blood expelled through salting or roasting, to which *mishrak sharik* can apply. Thus stacked meat and a drop of separate blood are not contradictory cases. [Shakh 76:4–5 and Taz 76:1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_עו_א).
