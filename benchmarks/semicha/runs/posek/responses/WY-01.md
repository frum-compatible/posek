The seller’s claim is overstated. **One may purchase and use properly identified, otherwise kosher aravos grown with artificial irrigation l’chatchilah.** There is a real basis for preferring river-grown aravos according to some authorities, but that preference should not be presented as the universally required purchasing standard or as proof that irrigated aravos are merely b’dieved.

The controlling ruling is the **Mechaber, Shulchan Arukh, Orach Chayim 647:1**. He explains that *arvei nachal* names a particular kind of willow, most of which grows beside streams, and rules: **“ואפילו היה גדל במדבר או בהרים כשר”**—“even if it grew in the desert or mountains, it is valid” (my translation). The river describes the species’ usual habitat; it is not an indispensable location for each individual tree. This wording belongs to the Mechaber, not to the Rema’s gloss about green stems. [Shulchan Arukh 647:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh,_Orach_Chayim.647.1)

That establishes validity, but the word *kasher* alone does not settle every question of preferred practice. The earlier sources explain why:

- **Mishnah and Gemara, Sukkah 33b:** the Mishnah validates an aravah *shel ba’al*. Rashi explains this as one growing in a field away from the river, with land adequately watered by rainfall. The ensuing baraisa expressly includes *shel ba’al* and *shel harim*, field-grown and mountain-grown willows. Nevertheless, **Rashi, s.v. “Arvei nachal hagedelos al hanachal,”** says there is a mitzvah preference for those growing by streams while allowing *shel ba’al*. Thus, validity and preference are already distinct in Rashi. [Sukkah 33b, Mishnah, Gemara and Rashi](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/סוכה_לג_ב)
- **Tosafos, Sukkah 34a, s.v. “Verabbanan lamikdash mena lehu,”** raises a stronger objection. If the plural *arvei* teaches that two willow branches are needed, it cannot also supply the separate inclusion of willows grown away from rivers. Tosafos therefore says to take care to use river-grown aravos. This is a genuine stringent position; it should not be erased from the discussion. [Tosafos, Sukkah 34a](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/סוכה_לד_א)
- **Rosh, Sukkah 3:13**, challenges that conclusion and observes that his teachers did not require river-grown branches. He explains *arvei nachal* as the **kind** that generally grows beside a river. Consequently, a specimen of that kind grown elsewhere is included without needing a separate derivation. He finds support in **Rambam, Hilchos Shofar, Sukkah veLulav 7:3**, whose species-based definition and express validation of desert- and mountain-grown specimens underlie the Shulchan Arukh’s language. [Rosh 3:13](https://wiki.jewishbooks.org.il/mediawiki/wiki/רא%22ש/סוכה/ג/יג), [Rambam 7:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Shofar,_Sukkah_and_Lulav.7.3)

**Tur, Orach Chayim 647**, explicitly reports both levels: a view requiring one to seek water-grown aravos initially, with other locations acceptable afterward, and the Rosh’s view permitting mountain-grown aravos initially as well. The seller is therefore presenting one side of a dispute as though it were the uncontested conclusion. [Tur 647](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/טור_אורח_חיים_תרמז)

The later authorities preserve that distinction. **Mishnah Berurah 647:3** reports that some say it is better initially to take river-grown aravos, but that the **Taz** implies one need not be particular about this. **Taz 647:2** goes further in explaining the established practice: using aravos from elsewhere demonstrates that they are valid. It would be inaccurate either to call river growth an obligatory standard for everyone or to say there is no source at all for preferring it. [Mishnah Berurah 647:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Berurah.647.3), [Taz 647:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Turei_Zahav_on_Shulchan_Arukh,_Orach_Chayim.647.2)

**Artificial irrigation needs a separate distinction.** Under the Rosh–Rambam approach, applied by the Shulchan Arukh, the means of supplying water does not turn an otherwise kosher willow into a b’dieved choice. That is an application of the species-based rule; these early texts do not discuss modern irrigation systems.

Nor should *shel ba’al* simply be translated as “artificially irrigated”: Rashi defines it as rainfall-sufficient land. **Arukh HaShulchan 647:5–7** expressly discusses *beis hashelachin*, land watered by a person. He records the practice of not insisting on river growth and additionally proposes that *nachal* includes a valley or level ground, not only a watercourse. His discussion does not establish a requirement to buy from a natural riverbank. [Arukh HaShulchan 647:5–7](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/ערוך_השולחן_אורח_חיים_תרמז)

A contemporary treatment directly addresses the remaining *hiddur* question. **R. Yosef Yona, “Zanei aravos vedin aravah she’einah gedeilah al hanachal ve’od,” HaOtzar, issue 57, Tishrei 5782, section ה, subsection on artificial irrigation**, suggests that if abundant water is the point of the preference, ample artificial irrigation may supply that advantage too. He leaves room for doubt under Tosafos’s stricter understanding. This is the author’s proposed reasoning, not proof that every authority equates irrigation with river growth. The basic permission to use irrigated aravos does not depend on accepting that proposal. [R. Yosef Yona’s article](https://www.ha-otzar.net/article/812)

For the purchaser, the practical order is:

1. Buy a reliably identified kosher aravah in good condition. Growing beside a river does not make a different species kosher. The Shulchan Arukh also addresses drying, substantial leaf loss and a severed top; Mishnah Berurah recommends seeking intact foliage where readily available. [Shulchan Arukh 647:1–2](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh,_Orach_Chayim.647.1), [Mishnah Berurah 647:11](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Berurah.647.11)
2. If equally good river-grown aravos are readily available, choosing them has a legitimate basis as a preference according to the stricter view.
3. A purchaser without an established contrary practice may choose good irrigated aravos l’chatchilah. The seller’s assertion alone supplies no universal obligation to pay a premium or reject them. Someone following a particular posek or established minhag concerning this preference should retain that distinction.
