**(a)** The subject is **חלב הקיבה**, milk the animal swallowed, recovered from its stomach. It must be distinguished from **עור הקיבה**, the stomach wall, which is meat.

The three principal positions are:

1. **Rif and Rambam:** Both clear milk, **צלול**, and coagulated milk, **קרוש**, are **פירשא בעלמא**—digested matter that no longer has the halachic status of milk. Consequently, meat may be cooked with it. Rif treats the baraita prohibiting a stomach cooked with its contents as belonging to the earlier ruling, before the retraction; Rambam explains that the contents have changed in the intestines. Sources: [Rif, Chullin 38b, in the discussion of כחל](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/רי%22ף_על_הש%22ס/חולין/פרק_ח); [Rambam, Ma’akhalot Asurot 9:15](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah%2C_Forbidden_Foods.9.15). The explicit clear/coagulated comparison appears in [Tur, Yoreh De‘ah 87, paragraph beginning חלב הנמצא בקיבה](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/טור_יורה_דעה_פז).
2. **Rashi:** Even coagulated contents remain actual milk; certainly clear contents do. The permission concerning contents of a nonkosher carcass does not prove that they lost their milk status: they came from outside the animal and are merely stored inside it. Rashi explicitly retracts his earlier permissive practice and prohibits contents salted with the stomach wall because of בשר בחלב. Source: [Rashi, Chullin 116b, s.v. הרי זו אסורה](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Chullin.116b.1.2).
3. **Rabbeinu Tam:** Distinguish the two states. Clear contents remain milk, while coagulated contents are פירשא. He thereby reconciles the prohibition of a stomach cooked with its milk with the concluding permission to use stomach contents as a coagulant. Tosafot adds that, on his reasoning, the coagulated contents remain permitted even after salting inside the stomach. Source: [Tosafot, Chullin 116b, s.v. הכי גרסינן ברוב ספרים](https://www.sefaria.org/Tosafot_on_Chullin.116b.16.1).

**Shulchan Arukh, YD 87:9**, states the Rif–Rambam permission as its first, anonymous ruling: “אינו חלב ומותר לבשל בו בשר אפילו בצלול שבה,” and then records a prohibiting opinion. **Rema**, “וכן נוהגין,” adopts the prohibition of clear contents; his distinction in 87:10 follows Rabbeinu Tam, rather than Rashi’s prohibition of coagulated contents too. Taz 87:7 and Shach 87:25 explicitly read the gloss this way. Shach nevertheless adds a *l’chatchilah* restriction even for coagulated contents because of מראית העין. Sources: [SA/Rema and Taz, 87:9](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_פז_ט); [Shach 87:25](https://www.sefaria.org/Siftei_Kohen_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.25.1).

**(b)** In **87:10**, the Mechaber prohibits using these contents to set cheese after they were salted inside the stomach or remained there for a full twenty-four hours: “אסור להעמיד בו.” Salting and prolonged soaking transfer taste—מליח כרותח and כבוש כמבושל. The difficulty is that, according to his preceding ruling, even clear contents are פירשא. Why should they now create a meat–milk prohibition? [Shulchan Arukh, YD 87:10](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.10).

**The answer concerns the absorbed meat taste, not a restoration of milk status.** The contents may remain פירשא but have absorbed taste from the stomach wall. Introducing that taste into fresh milk is the concern. Thus the *l’chatchilah* prohibition applies to both clear and coagulated contents. The Beit Yosef cites Rabbeinu Yerucham’s stringent view on this basis and adopts it initially, while permitting the resulting cheese *b’diavad* in reliance on the lenient authorities. Shach explains that this is the Mechaber’s position; the Rema’s treatment of clear contents is stricter. Sources: [Beit Yosef, YD 87, passage ט–י, beginning ומשמע מדברי רבינו and its conclusion ולענין הלכה](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/טור_יורה_דעה_פז); [Shach 87:29](https://www.sefaria.org/Siftei_Kohen_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.29.1).

There is a further explanatory distinction: the *b’diavad* leniency for coagulated contents is **not ordinary נ״ט בר נ״ט דהיתרא**. Shach challenges that explanation: meat → stomach contents → fresh milk supplies only one transfer before the meat taste encounters milk. Compare meat → bowl → fish, where two transfers occur before the fish meets dairy. His explanation is that this particular פירשא lacks the capacity to carry effective meat taste into the new milk; its degraded status makes the taste weaker than the ordinary נ״ט בר נ״ט case. Source: [Shach 87:31](https://www.sefaria.org/Siftei_Kohen_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.31.1).

**(c)** Rema rules that if cheese was already made with the **clear** contents prohibited through salting or soaking, it needs **sixty times their quantity** in the milk being set. The comparison is against the prohibited stomach **milk**, not against the stomach wall. Clear contents count as milk; absorbing meat taste makes the milk forbidden through בשר בחלב and חתיכה נעשית נבילה. With coagulated contents, the resulting cheese is permitted *b’diavad* even without sixty. Sources: [Rema 87:10](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.10); [Shach 87:30](https://www.sefaria.org/Siftei_Kohen_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.30.1).

**Shach’s challenge:** Why does sixty suffice? An intrinsically forbidden **דבר המעמיד** is not nullified even in a thousand, as 87:11 states; בשר בחלב is ordinarily treated as איסורו מחמת עצמו. Here the forbidden contents actually set the cheese. [SA/Rema 87:11](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.11).

**His two resolutions for the Rema are:**

1. The rule against nullification applies to a coagulant intrinsically forbidden in its own substance, such as neveilah. Here its prohibition results from the combination of meat and milk, so sixty can suffice.
2. Even if genuine, cooked בשר בחלב counts as intrinsically forbidden for this purpose, this case arose only through **salting or soaking**, which creates a rabbinic meat–milk prohibition. The Rema may therefore decline to apply the stringent, any-quantity rule of מעמיד to it.

Shach ends “ודוחק קצת”: this defense retains some difficulty. He also mentions a separate explanation for the **Tur**—it may follow the position that even a forbidden coagulant can be nullified in sixty. That cannot simply explain the Rema, who expressly rules otherwise in 87:11. Source: [Shach 87:30](https://www.sefaria.org/Siftei_Kohen_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.30.1).
