**CR-03**

The essential distinction is between **דם פליטה**, blood emerging in the normal process of salting or roasting, and **דם בעין**, separate blood deposited on the meat. “Blood from another piece” does not by itself identify which category applies.

**(a) Three explanations for permitting stacked pieces**

The starting point is **Ḥullin 113a**, the discussion of Rav Sheshet salting one piece at a time and the conclusion **אלא לא שנא**. Rashi, s.v. *אלא לא שנא*, explains that pieces occupied with expelling do not absorb, and they finish expelling together. Tosafot question how that explains pieces salted successively, when the bottom piece finishes first. [Gemara and Rashi, Ḥullin 113a](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חולין_קיג_א)

Three principal approaches answer that difficulty:

1. **Rabbenu Tam: דם מישרק שריק.** Blood discharged in salting slides away through the drainage rather than becoming retained in another meat piece. Rashba records Rabbenu Tam permitting new meat to be salted over meat salted even several days earlier. This approach therefore does not require the receiving piece still to be expelling its own blood or ציר. It does require the ordinary draining situation; meat submerged in accumulated blood in a nonperforated vessel is different. Fish are also different because their soft tissue absorbs readily. **Practical difference:** a lower piece that has already finished all its own discharge can still be permitted under this explanation. **Source:** Rashba, *Ḥiddushim*, Ḥullin 112b, s.v. *דגים ועופות*, paragraph beginning *ור״ת ז״ל התיר אפילו לאחר כמה ימים*. [Rashba’s text](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חולין_קיב_ב#חידושי_הרשב%22א)

2. **R. Yosef of Orléans: uninterrupted absorption and expulsion.** Once the lower piece’s channels are open, blood reaching it before its discharge stops is expelled in turn; the continuing flow prevents closure. Successive pieces can prolong that uninterrupted process. This is an application of **כבולעו כך פולטו**, rather than a claim that nothing enters. **Practical differences:** a break after the lower piece has finished discharging defeats this explanation; conversely, an uninterrupted chain can continue beyond the lower piece’s original discharge. The lower piece must wait for the upper piece to complete its required salting period before being taken for cooking. Tosafot also record a related explanation: the lower piece’s continuing **ציר** carries out blood absorbed from above. That version depends on its own continuing discharge of ציר, whereas R. Yosef emphasizes uninterrupted flow. **Source:** Tosafot, Ḥullin 112b, s.v. *ודגים רפו קרמייהו ופלטי ועופות קמיטי*. [Exact Tosafot passage](https://www.sefaria.org/Tosafot_on_Chullin.112b.4.1?lang=he)

3. **Continued discharge prevents absorption: איידי דטריד למיפלט ציר לא בלע דם.** Although the lower piece finishes expelling blood first, it continues expelling ציר much longer; during that period it does not absorb the upper piece’s blood. This is the final explanation in that Tosafot and the explanation adopted by **Tur and Shulchan Arukh, YD 70:1**. **Practical difference:** unlike explanation 2, the lower piece need not await completion of the upper piece’s salting once its own required salting is complete, since it has absorbed no new blood. Unlike Rabbenu Tam’s approach, protection ends when its own discharge of ציר ends. [Tur, YD 70, opening paragraph](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/טור_יורה_דעה_ע); [Shulchan Arukh 70:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.70.1?lang=he)

These explanations also distinguish a natural depression in a piece from immersion in a pool that prevents drainage. The Mechaber permits ציר collecting in depressions between the pieces. Rema nevertheless requires turning a piece with a receptacle-like cavity downward **לכתחילה**, while permitting it **בדיעבד**. This should not be expanded into permission to salt in a nonperforated container. The detailed comparison of the explanations is collected in **Beit Yosef, YD 70, s.v. מולחין הרבה חתיכות**. [Beit Yosef 70:1:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Beit_Yosef%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.70.1.1?lang=he)

**(b) Separate cold blood falling during salting**

Assume the meat is undergoing effective kashering salting and the droplet is **דם בעין**. **Rema, YD 70:6**, expressly rules that the meat becomes prohibited: neither **איידי דטריד לפלוט לא בלע** nor **כבולעו כך פולטו** is applied to this blood. Continuing the salting or simply salting again does not supply the remedy available for certain cases of absorbed ציר. The fact that the receiving meat is still expelling its own blood is therefore insufficient. [Rema 70:6](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.70.6?lang=he)

The blood’s being physically cold does not prevent absorption: the meat is halachically hot through **מליח כרותח**. Nor does **טהור מליח וטמא תפל** permit it. Rema distinguishes an unsalted solid, from which the salted piece does not draw out forbidden material, from an already available forbidden **liquid**: a moist salted piece absorbs the latter. **Source: YD 91:5**, Rema, *והא דטהור מלוח וטמא תפל שרי*. [Text](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.91.5?lang=he)

The extent of the prohibition follows the respective general rules: the Mechaber’s ordinary rule for nonfatty absorption through salting is **כדי קליפה**, removal of a thin surface layer. Rema’s customary rule requires **ששים**, with the applicable local removal as well; without sufficient permitted meat, merely peeling does not generally permit the piece. This application to the droplet is a synthesis of the particular ruling in 70:6 and the general rules in **105:9**, not an explicit separate droplet ruling by the Mechaber in 70:6. [Shulchan Arukh and Rema 105:9](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.105.9?lang=he)

Do not read “during its salting period” as automatically permitting a later occurrence. **Shakh 70:44**, referring to **91:5**, explains that treating the meat as no longer hot after the required salting period is a limited leniency involving both substantial loss and a סעודת מצוה; ordinarily it remains treated as hot while the relevant salting condition persists. [Shakh 70:44:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Siftei_Kohen_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.70.44.1?lang=he)

**(c) Separate cold blood falling during roasting**

**Shulchan Arukh, YD 76:1:** roasting ordinarily needs no prior salting because the fire draws out the meat’s own blood—**נורא משאב שאיב**. But separate blood dripping onto the roast is not covered by that principle, even when the blood is cold. The Mechaber requires **כדי נטילה**, removing approximately a finger’s thickness at the affected location, rather than merely a thin קליפה. [Shulchan Arukh 76:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.76.1?lang=he); [Taz 76:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Turei_Zahav_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.76.1?lang=he)

There are two distinct reasons to state:

- **Why the cold droplet is absorbed:** **תתאה גבר**—the hot roast beneath heats the cold blood falling onto it. Shakh adds an alternative: while the roast is on the fire, this is treated as hot entering hot. **Shakh 76:4.**
- **Why roasting does not remove it permissibly:** the special rule concerns blood emerging with ציר through salting or roasting, not free-standing external blood. This distinction is explicit in **Tosafot, Ḥullin 113a, s.v. אין מחזיקין דם בבני מעיים**, and in Beit Yosef’s quotation of the Rosh at **YD 76, s.v. אבל דם אחר**. [Shakh 76:4:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Siftei_Kohen_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.76.4.1?lang=he); [Tosafot](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חולין_קיג_א#תוספות); [Beit Yosef 76:2:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Beit_Yosef%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.76.2.1?lang=he)

Rema writes no gloss at 76:1. Nevertheless, **Shakh 76:5** explicitly explains that Rema relies on his general practice of requiring **sixty** for salting and roasting; his silence does not mean agreement that נטילה alone always suffices. Thus, for that practice, without sixty against the blood the piece is prohibited. Shakh **76:3** also extends the issue to blood that falls onto raw meat and is then roasted without first being washed off. [Shakh 76:3–5, displayed with the סעיף](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_עו_א#ש%22ך_-_שפתי_כהן)
