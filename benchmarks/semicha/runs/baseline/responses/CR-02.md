**(a) Three positions and the codified ruling.** Here חלב הקיבה means milk the animal swallowed and which is recovered from its stomach; עור הקיבה means the actual stomach wall. They must not be conflated.

1. **Rif and Rambam:** Both clear/liquid milk (צלול) and curdled milk (קרוש) in the stomach are פירשא בעלמא—digestive waste, no longer halakhically milk. Therefore one may cook meat with them. Rambam expressly permits a stomach cooked with its contents because the contents have changed in the digestive system. Elsewhere he distinguishes permissible stomach contents from the prohibited stomach wall of an otherwise prohibited animal. See Rambam, *Ma’akhalot Asurot* [9:15](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah%2C_Forbidden_Foods.9.15) and [4:19](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah%2C_Forbidden_Foods.4.19); Rif’s position is expressly recorded in [Tur, YD 87, paragraph beginning חלב הנמצא בקיבה](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/טור_יורה_דעה_פז).
2. **Rashi:** Even curdled contents retain the status of milk; clear contents certainly do. Consequently, salting the contents with the stomach wall can create prohibited בשר בחלב. His discussion expressly rejects his earlier lenient practice and the proposed analogy to נותן טעם בר נותן טעם. See [Rashi, Chullin 116b, s.v. הרי זו אסורה](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חולין_קטז_ב#רש%22י).
3. **Rabbeinu Tam:** Clear contents remain milk, while contents already curdled in the stomach are פירשא. Thus the prohibition applies to the clear contents, but not to the curdled contents as milk. See [Tosafot, Chullin 116b, passage beginning הכי גרסינן ברוב ספרים](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חולין_קטז_ב#תוספות), and Tur YD 87. The Tur identifies this as the Rosh’s conclusion, although Beit Yosef there questions that reading of the Rosh and thinks he inclines toward Rashi.

**Shulchan Arukh, YD 87:9**, states the Rif–Rambam position anonymously, permitting cooking even with the clear contents, and then records a dissenting view. **Rema** says the custom follows the prohibition; Shach §25 and Taz §7 explain that this means Rabbeinu Tam’s distinction, rather than Rashi’s prohibition of both forms. Shach separately raises מראית עין against cooking meat with even curdled contents initially; that does not make them intrinsically milk. [SA/Rema and commentaries, 87:9](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_פז_ט).

Rema 87:10 also distinguishes contents curdled originally from contents recovered clear and subsequently curdled: the latter ordinarily retain the clear-milk classification, although he allows reliance on the lenient position in a case of loss. [Rema 87:10](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.10).

**(b) Why does Shulchan Arukh prohibit using salted or soaked contents to set cheese?** In **87:10**, the Mechaber forbids using contents salted with their stomach wall, or left there for a full twenty-four hours, to set cheese. The latter is כבוש כמבושל; the former involves halakhically effective מליחה, not merely any sprinkling of salt. Shach §§27–28 discusses those qualifications. The difficulty is that **87:9** treats even clear contents as פירשא: how can contact with meat create בשר בחלב in something that is not milk?

The answer distinguishes **the contents’ own status** from **meat flavor absorbed from the wall**. Although the contents are not milk, salting or soaking enables them to absorb meat flavor. Introducing them into fresh, ordinary milk can therefore introduce meat flavor into dairy. Thus the initial prohibition applies even to curdled contents. It need not mean that the contents themselves already became prohibited meat-and-milk while in the stomach. This is **Shach 87:29**, following **Beit Yosef, YD 87, s.v. ומשמע מדברי רבינו דלסברת הרי״ף והרמב״ם**. Beit Yosef nevertheless permits the cheese after the fact on the strength of the lenient authorities; Shach explains the Mechaber’s formulation accordingly. [Shach §29](https://www.sefaria.org/Siftei_Kohen_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.29.1); [Beit Yosef, YD 87](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/טור_יורה_דעה_פז#בית_יוסף).

Why can the curdled case nevertheless be permitted after the fact without sixty? **Shach §31** rejects treating this as ordinary נ״ט בר נ״ט דהיתרא. In the familiar example, meat flavors a vessel, which flavors fish, before dairy enters. Here the stomach wall flavors the contents directly, and the next transfer is already into milk. The lenient explanation is instead that the contents’ degraded, waste-like character prevents them from carrying effective meat flavor into the new cheese. Beit Yosef describes this as weaker than נ״ט בר נ״ט because of the פירשא; Darkhei Moshe says that the meat flavor absorbed in the פירשא is itself treated as פירשא. These are explanations based on degradation, not permission for every food that has absorbed meat flavor. [Shach 87:31](https://www.sefaria.org/Siftei_Kohen_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.31.1); [Darkhei Moshe, YD 87:8](https://wiki.jewishbooks.org.il/mediawiki/wiki/דרכי_משה/יורה_דעה/פז).

**(c) Shach’s challenge, his two defenses, and Rema’s explanation of sixty.** Rema 87:10 permits cheese made with the prohibited clear contents when the fresh milk contains sixty against those contents. **Shach 87:30** clarifies the denominator: it is the **stomach milk that became prohibited**, not merely an estimated quantity of meat flavor or the stomach wall. Clear milk that absorbs the wall’s meat flavor becomes prohibited through בשר בחלב, with חתיכה נעשית נבילה.

His difficulty is that this very point appears to make the contents an intrinsically prohibited substance. Since they are also a **דבר המעמיד**, an ingredient that sets the cheese, why should sixty suffice? Rema **87:11** says an intrinsically prohibited coagulant is not nullified even in a thousand. Shach compares the treatment of בשר בחלב as איסור מחמת עצמו for חתיכה הראויה להתכבד in **YD 101:2**. [Shach 87:30](https://www.sefaria.org/Siftei_Kohen_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.30.1); [SA/Rema 87:11](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.11).

He offers two progressively narrower defenses:

1. **Intrinsic prohibition versus prohibition through combination:** The non-nullification rule may apply only to an ingredient forbidden in itself, such as נבילה. Here the coagulant was permitted material and became forbidden through the combination of meat and milk; for this purpose it may still be nullified in sixty.
2. **Rabbinic versus cooked meat-and-milk:** Even if meat and milk actually cooked together become a single intrinsically forbidden substance whose coagulating effect prevents nullification, the present prohibition arose through salting or soaking. It is only rabbinic, and Rema may therefore decline to give it that stronger status for דבר המעמיד.

These are **Shach’s proposed reconciliations**, not an assertion that all rabbinic coagulants are nullified. He ends by acknowledging some difficulty. He also notes that the Tur could theoretically follow Rabbeinu Tam’s broader view that even a prohibited coagulant is nullified in sixty; that alone cannot explain Rema, who states the opposite rule in 87:11. [Shach 87:30](https://www.sefaria.org/Siftei_Kohen_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.30.1).

**Rema’s own Darkhei Moshe 87:8** supplies additional context. He first invokes the distinction between an intrinsically forbidden coagulant and meat-and-milk, whose prohibition depends on imparting flavor. He then gives another explanation, citing Rabbeinu Baruch through Mordechai: ordinarily the setting material contains **both clear and curdled contents**. The clear portion is prohibited; the curdled portion is permissible. The setting therefore involves **זה וזה גורם**, permitted and prohibited causes together. That removes the special obstacle of דבר המעמיד, but **sixty is still necessary against the prohibited clear portion** to remove its ordinary prohibition. It does not establish that sixty always suffices when an intrinsically forbidden ingredient is the sole coagulant. Rema’s express זה וזה גורם qualification in 87:11 fits this account. [Darkhei Moshe 87:8](https://wiki.jewishbooks.org.il/mediawiki/wiki/דרכי_משה/יורה_דעה/פז); [Rema 87:11](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.11).
