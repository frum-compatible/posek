The seller’s claim is too categorical. **Genuine aravos grown away from a river—including ordinarily cultivated, artificially irrigated aravos—may be purchased and used לכתחילה according to the accepted ruling followed by major poskim.** There is a real opinion favoring river-grown aravos, but it should be described as a disputed preference, not as a universally binding rule that cultivated aravos are only בדיעבד. This assumes that the branches are the correct species and otherwise kosher.

The Torah calls this species **ערבי נחל** ([Vayikra 23:40](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/ויקרא_כג_מ)). The Mishnah expressly permits **של בעל**, and the baraisa includes both **של בעל ושל הרים**—willows growing in rain-fed land and in the mountains. Importantly, Rashi defines *baal* as land sustained by rainfall without requiring irrigation; it is not the technical term for an artificially irrigated plantation. The relevant sugya is [Sukkah 33b, Mishnah and concluding Gemara, with Rashi s.v. ושל בעל and בעל](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/סוכה_לג_ב).

The central explanation is that **ערבי נחל identifies a botanical kind, rather than requiring every individual tree to stand beside a river**. Rambam states that this kind usually grows beside streams, which explains its biblical name, but a specimen grown in a desert or mountains is nevertheless valid. Shulchan Aruch adopts this explanation and ruling. Conversely, proximity to a river cannot make a different, invalid species kosher. See [Rambam, Hilchos Shofar, Sukkah veLulav 7:3–4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/רמב%22ם_הלכות_שופר_וסוכה_ולולב_ז) and [Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chayim 647:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Orach_Chayim.647.1).

There is nevertheless a significant dispute about preferred practice:

- **Rashi, Sukkah 33b, s.v. ערבי נחל הגדילות על הנחל**, says that the mitzvah is preferably performed with river-grown willows, while recognizing the validity of *shel baal*. This gives a genuine basis for seeking river-grown branches as a preference. [Rashi’s text](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/סוכה_לג_ב).
- **Tosafos, Sukkah 34a, s.v. ורבנן למקדש מנא להו**, takes a stricter approach: since the plural **ערבי** supplies the requirement for two willow branches, it cannot also supply the inclusion of non-river growth; Tosafos therefore cautions against taking aravos unless they grew beside a stream. This stringent strand must not be erased from the source history. [Tosafos’s text](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/סוכה_לד_א).
- **Rosh, Sukkah 3:13**, challenges that approach, observes that neither common practice nor his teachers insisted on river-grown aravos, and explains that the phrase names the species normally growing by rivers. Non-river specimens are already included in that species, without needing an additional derivation. [Rosh’s text, siman 13](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/רבינו_אשר_על_הש%22ס/סוכה/פרק_ג#סימן_יג).
- **Tur, Orach Chayim 647**, explicitly distinguishes the approaches: he reports that most commentators require seeking water-grown branches initially and validate other growth afterward, but that the Rosh permits mountain-grown aravos **לכתחילה**; he understands Rambam similarly. Consequently, the word “כשר” in the later codification should not automatically be read as “only after the fact.” [Tur’s text](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/טור_אורח_חיים_תרמז).

The later authorities retain that distinction. **Taz 647:2** goes beyond merely excusing non-river aravos: he explains a positive reason for taking them—to demonstrate the validity of the less obvious case. **Mishnah Berurah 647:3** summarizes that some consider river-grown branches preferable initially, but that the Taz indicates there is no need to be particular about this. It does not issue an unqualified instruction to buy only river-grown aravos. See [Taz 647:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Turei_Zahav_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Orach_Chayim.647.2) and [Mishnah Berurah 647:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Berurah.647.3).

Artificial irrigation is also addressed explicitly in **Aruch HaShulchan, Orach Chayim 647:5–7**. Section 5 describes the established practice as following the species-based explanation, with no intrinsic advantage on that account to an individual river-grown branch. Sections 6–7 discuss **בית השלחין**, land watered by a person, alongside rain-fed land; artificial watering is not introduced as a new disqualification. He additionally suggests that **נחל** can denote a valley or level lowland, broadening the literal reading beyond a riverbank. That last explanation is his proposed reconciliation, not a condition one must establish before relying on Shulchan Aruch. [Aruch HaShulchan’s text](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/ערוך_השולחן_אורח_חיים_תרמז#סימן_תרמז_סעיף_ה).

For purchasing, the practical consequences are:

1. **Do not reject otherwise kosher irrigated aravos because of the seller’s blanket claim.** The permissive position allows an initial purchase and use; it is not merely a rescue after someone has already performed the mitzvah.
2. **A person who follows the river-grown preference may reasonably choose those branches when otherwise suitable.** That preference has sources, but the seller should identify it as such. The sources do not support presenting everyone else’s aravos as universally second-class.
3. **Inspect what actually determines the branches’ kashrus and condition.** Ensure that they are genuine aravos, not צפצפה, and choose fresh, well-preserved branches with an intact stem tip and leaves. River provenance cannot compensate for an invalid species, a severed stem tip, or disqualifying leaf loss or dryness. These physical requirements are addressed in **Shulchan Aruch 647:1–2**; **Mishnah Berurah 647:11** recommends seeking intact, unwithered branches when readily available. [Shulchan Aruch](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_אורח_חיים_תרמז), [Mishnah Berurah](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/משנה_ברורה_על_אורח_חיים_תרמז).

Thus, riverbank origin can inform a purchaser’s chosen הידור or established practice, but it should not be allowed to obscure the validity—and substantial לכתחילה basis—of properly cultivated aravos.
