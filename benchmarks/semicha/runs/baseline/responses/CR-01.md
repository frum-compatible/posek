**(a) Poultry cooked with milk: eating, cooking, and benefit are separate questions.**

The three occurrences of לא תבשל גדי בחלב אמו yield separate prohibitions of אכילה, הנאה, and בישול (Chullin 115b). Thus, where the Torah prohibition applies, cooking for someone else does not avoid it. The Mishnah, Chullin 113a, records R. Akiva’s exclusion of חיה ועוף from the Torah prohibition. Chullin 116a distinguishes R. Akiva, who prohibits poultry with milk rabbinically, from R. Yose HaGelili, who permits poultry even rabbinically; the latter’s locality actually ate poultry with milk. These are distinct tannaitic positions, not a general permission to eat chicken Parmesan. [Chullin 115b:6](https://www.sefaria.org/Chullin.115b.6), [113a:18–19](https://www.sefaria.org/Chullin.113a.18-19), [116a:13–14](https://www.sefaria.org/Chullin.116a.13-14).

The principal Rishonim divide as follows:

- **Rif, as understood by Rosh, and Rambam:** The halachah follows R. Akiva. Rosh explains that Rif understands the anonymous first opinion of the Mishnah as compatible with R. Akiva: the latter explains the exclusions, rather than opposing a majority. Rambam explicitly rules that poultry and chayah with milk are prohibited to eat only מדרבנן, but **permitted to cook and to benefit from**. [Rosh, Chullin 8:51:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Rosh_on_Chullin.8.51.1); [Rambam, Ma’achalot Asurot 9:4](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah%2C_Forbidden_Foods.9.4).
- **Tosafot’s stringent position:** On Chullin 113a, s.v. *Basar behemah tehorah*, Tosafot understand the Rabbis as including poultry in the Torah prohibition. On 104b, s.v. *Of u-gevinah*, they suggest that the custom to wait after poultry reflects rejection of Agra’s rabbinic classification. On this approach, cooking and benefit are prohibited as well. However, the latter passage presents an explanation of custom, not an entirely unqualified final ruling. [Tosafot 113a:18:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Tosafot_on_Chullin.113a.18.1), [104b:12:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Tosafot_on_Chullin.104b.12.1).

An apparent difficulty for Rambam is Rav Ashi’s formulation, “all meat is forbidden to cook in milk,” some biblically and some rabbinically (Chullin 104a). **Maggid Mishneh 9:4** explains that here “to cook” means cooking for consumption: the Mishnah uses the Torah’s expression for the eating prohibition. Rambam does not extend the poultry decree to the independent acts of cooking or benefit. Shulchan Aruch adopts this permission in **YD 87:3**. Later disagreement should not be mistaken for unanimity among Rishonim: Shakh 87:2,4 defends the permissive ruling against the stringent readings of Maharshal and Bach. [Chullin 104a:7](https://www.sefaria.org/Chullin.104a.7); [Maggid Mishneh 9:4:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Maggid_Mishneh_on_Mishneh_Torah%2C_Forbidden_Foods.9.4.1); [YD 87:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.3); [Shakh, YD 87](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/ש%22ך_על_יורה_דעה_פז).

**(b) Agra permits a sequence of eating, not a poultry-and-cheese mixture.**

Agra, R. Abba’s father-in-law, taught: עוף וגבינה נאכלין באפיקורן, explained as without washing the hands or cleansing the mouth between them. The ensuing episode concerns Rav Yitzchak eating cheese and then meat without washing his hands. [Chullin 104b:11–13](https://www.sefaria.org/Chullin.104b.11-13).

**Tosafot** initially read Agra as allowing even poultry **before** cheese. Their inference concerning ordinary meat applies to cheese-first: meat-first would require a later meal, not merely washing and cleansing. They also present Rabbeinu Tam/Behag’s alternative: washing and cleansing allow cheese after meat immediately; after cheese, mouth-cleansing is unnecessary, though handwashing remains required. Mar Ukva’s waiting then reflects omitted cleansing or personal stringency. Nevertheless, Tosafot record the custom against immediate cheese after meat, including poultry. Rabbeinu Tam explains poultry’s special treatment by its not adhering to hands, teeth, and gums. [Tosafot, Chullin 104b, s.v. *Of u-gevinah ein, basar u-gevinah lo*](https://www.sefaria.org/Tosafot_on_Chullin.104b.12.1).

**Rambam** restricts Agra to **cheese/milk first, poultry afterward**. In that direction no קינוח הפה or נטילת ידים is required. After poultry, however, one waits approximately six hours, just as after animal meat, because meat remains between the teeth. Moreover, cheese followed by **chayah** requires the cleansing applicable to behemah, although Rambam classifies chayah-with-milk as rabbinic too. [Ma’achalot Asurot 9:26–28](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah%2C_Forbidden_Foods.9.26-28).

**Beit Yosef, YD 89, opening discussion**, identifies two independent axes of disagreement:

- **Order:** Ramban, as quoted there through Rashba and Ran, permits Agra’s leniency even after poultry; Rambam permits only cheese followed by poultry.
- **Species:** Ran extends the leniency to chayah under R. Akiva. Rashba’s practical conclusion is cheese followed by poultry **or chayah**, while rejecting immediate cheese after either. Rambam keeps chayah with behemah. Beit Yosef reports the suggested explanation that chayah resembles behemah; he also quotes Rabbeinu Tam’s adhesion explanation and Rosh’s explanation that poultry is simply more common.

Consequently, Beit Yosef explains why Tur attributes this rule specifically to **Rambam**, although Agra’s statement is already in the Gemara: Rambam’s formulation excludes both the broader ordering allowed by Ramban and the broader species coverage allowed by Rashba. Shulchan Aruch’s basic ruling follows these Rambam distinctions in **89:1–2**; Rema’s additional hard-cheese practice is a separate qualification. [Beit Yosef, YD 89:1:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Beit_Yosef%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.89.1.1); [Shulchan Aruch, YD 89:1–2](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.89.1-2).

**(c) Cooking meat for a non-Jew in a dairy pot that is not ben-yomo.**

Assume an ordinary, non-sharp dish; a pot thoroughly clean of actual dairy residue; and a full twenty-four hours since its relevant dairy use. Its absorbed taste is נותן טעם לפגם. This differs from cooking meat with actual milk, which a Jew may not do merely because a non-Jew will eat it. Also, the ordinary ban on deliberately cooking food for Jewish consumption in an unkashered, non-ben-yomo pot remains in force. [YD 103:5](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.103.5), [122:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.122.2).

**Pitchei Teshuvah 87:8**, citing **Chamudei Daniel §18**, gives a basis to permit this case: the remaining prohibition is rabbinic, and cooking and benefit were not prohibited; ordinary cookware does not reveal whether it is dairy, so there is no apparent מראית עין. But the entry then expressly adds **“מ״מ יש להחמיר”**, invoking Rema **87:6**, who prohibits stoking a non-Jew’s pot because it may cook meat with absorbed milk. It suggests extra stringency because the absorption originally was actual milk. Therefore, citing this entry as an unqualified permission omits its conclusion. [Pitchei Teshuvah 87:8](https://www.sefaria.org/Pitchei_Teshuva_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.8); [Rema, YD 87:6](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De'ah.87.6).

**Ben Ish Chai, year II, Beha’alotekha §11**, explicitly permits cooking meat for a non-Jew in such a dairy pot. He nevertheless prohibits the reverse case when a conspicuously large meat pot is used for dairy, because its appearance creates מראית עין. Thus the answer is **permitted according to this ruling under the stated assumptions, but subject to the lechatchilah stringency recorded in Pitchei Teshuvah—not an undisputed blanket permission**. [Ben Ish Chai, II, Beha’alotekha 11](https://ashoova.co.il/ask/בן-איש-חי-הלכות-ב-בהעלותך-י״א/amp/).
