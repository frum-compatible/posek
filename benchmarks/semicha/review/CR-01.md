Maggid Mishneh explains the apparent cooking-language objection as referring to eating; the answer correctly identifies whose interpretation this is. Sources: [Rambam, Forbidden Foods 9:4](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah%2C_Forbidden_Foods.9.4), [Rosh, Chullin 8:51:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Rosh_on_Chullin.8.51.1), [Chullin 104a:7](https://www.sefaria.org/Chullin.104a.7), [Maggid Mishneh, Forbidden Foods 9:4:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Maggid_Mishneh_on_Mishneh_Torah%2C_Forbidden_Foods.9.4.1).

Shakh explicitly says that inference is not compelled and offers Rambam’s interpretation of Agra. The answer preserves that qualification rather than treating the custom explanation as an unequivocal Tosafot ruling. Sources: [Tosafot, Chullin 113a:18:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Tosafot_on_Chullin.113a.18.1), [Tosafot, Chullin 104b, s.v. Of u-gevinah ein](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חולין_קד_ב#תוספות), [Shakh YD 87:4:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Siftei_Kohen_on_Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De%27ah.87.4.1), [Shulchan Arukh YD 87:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De%27ah.87.3).

Sources: [Chullin 104b and Tosafot, s.v. Of u-gevinah ein](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חולין_קד_ב), [Rambam, Forbidden Foods 9:26–28](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah%2C_Forbidden_Foods.9.26-28).
