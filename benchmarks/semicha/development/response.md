**(a) The three positions and the ruling**

The case concerns milk the animal **ingested**, subsequently recovered from its stomach. Distinguish the contents, חלב הקיבה, from the stomach wall, עור הקיבה, which is meat.

| Position | Clear contents — צלול | Curdled contents — קרוש |
|---|---|---|
| **Rif and Rambam** | פירשא בעלמא: no longer halakhic milk | Also פירשא |
| **Rashi** | Remains milk | Also remains milk |
| **Rabbeinu Tam** | Remains milk | Becomes פירשא |

Thus Rif/Rambam permit cooking meat with either form; Rashi forbids both; Rabbeinu Tam distinguishes. The Rif’s position is verified here through **Tur, Yoreh De‘ah 87**, rather than an independent reading of the Rif. Rambam expressly permits a stomach cooked with its contents because their alteration in the digestive tract makes them refuse rather than milk: **Hilkhot Ma’akhalot Asurot 9:15**. [Tur 87](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/טור_יורה_דעה_פז), [Rambam 9:15](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/רמב%22ם_הלכות_מאכלות_אסורות_ט_טו).

The printed **Rashi, Chullin 116b, s.v. הרי זו אסורה**, explicitly retracts his earlier permissive practice. The contents’ independence from the animal’s body does not remove their milk status. If salted with the stomach wall, they become prohibited meat-and-milk; subsequent transfer into cheesemaking milk is not ordinary permitted נותן טעם בר נותן טעם. **Tosafot there, s.v. כאן לאחר חזרה**, records Rabbeinu Tam’s reconciliation: statements treating stomach milk as פירשא concern curdled contents, whereas the prohibitions concern clear milk. [Chullin 116b, Rashi and Tosafot](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חולין_קטז_ב).

**Shulchan Arukh, YD 87:9**, opens with the Rif/Rambam position, including clear contents, then records ויש מי שאוסר. **Rema** adds וכן נוהגין. Shach **87:25** and Taz **87:7** explain that the latter position concerns clear milk, following Rabbeinu Tam; Rema’s distinction in 87:10 confirms this. Shach separately recommends avoiding even curdled contents with meat *l’chatchilah* because of מראית עין; that is distinct from their substantive classification as milk. [Shulchan Arukh 87:9 and commentaries](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_פז_ט).

**(b) Why forbid using the salted or soaked contents?**

**Shulchan Arukh 87:10** forbids setting cheese with contents salted in the stomach or left there for a full twenty-four hours. The relevant absorption mechanisms are מליח כרותח and כבוש כמבושל. The difficulty is obvious: after 87:9 treats even clear contents as פירשא, where is the milk-and-meat prohibition?

The answer distinguishes **the contents’ own milk status** from **meat flavor absorbed from the wall**. Even if the contents are פירשא, introducing their absorbed meat flavor into fresh cheesemaking milk raises a new meat-and-milk concern. Beit Yosef adopts this concern *l’chatchilah*, while allowing an already-produced cheese *b’dieved* on the permissive view. On that view, the degraded contents do not effectively carry meat flavor onward to prohibit the cheese. See **Beit Yosef, YD 87, discussion of se‘ifim 9–10**, ending ולענין הלכה נראה. [Beit Yosef, on Tur 87](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/טור_יורה_דעה_פז).

Shach **87:29, 31** explains this distinction and rejects an ordinary נ״ט בר נ״ט calculation: wall → contents → cheese does not provide two permitted transfers before encountering milk. The special heter depends on פירשא. [Shach to 87:10](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_פז_י).

Rema’s *b’dieved* ruling differs from the Mechaber’s: curdled contents do not prohibit the cheese even without sixty, whereas clear contents require sixty. Clear contents that curdled subsequently ordinarily retain the stricter status, although Rema allows reliance on the lenient opinion in a case of loss. [Rema, 87:10](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Yoreh_De%27ah.87.10).

**(c) Shach’s challenge, its resolutions, and Rema’s explanation**

**Shach 87:30** asks why sixty suffices. The clear contents already became forbidden meat-and-milk through absorption, with חתיכה נעשית נבילה. Since they now set the cheese, they seem to be a דבר המעמיד prohibited intrinsically, which should not be nullified even in a thousand.

He offers two levels of resolution:

1. Non-nullification applies to something intrinsically forbidden, such as נבילה; here the prohibition results from a meat-and-milk mixture.
2. Even if genuinely **cooked** meat-and-milk counts as intrinsically forbidden, this case involves only salting or soaking. Its prohibition is rabbinic, so Rema can decline to assign it that stringent status for דבר המעמיד.

Shach acknowledges some difficulty. He also gives a separate possible explanation for the **Tur**: it may follow Rabbeinu Tam’s position that even a forbidden מעמיד can be nullified in sixty. That alone cannot explain Rema’s general rule in 87:11. [Shach 87:30](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_פז_י).

**Rema’s own discussion, Darkhei Moshe, YD 87:8**, supplies two explanations. First, meat-and-milk depends on flavor, unlike an independently forbidden coagulant. Second, following Rabbeinu Barukh as quoted in Mordekhai, the usual coagulant includes **both** prohibited clear contents and permitted curdled contents: זה וזה גורם. The permitted contribution removes the special non-nullification of an exclusively forbidden מעמיד, but ordinary nullification still requires sixty against the clear portion. [Darkhei Moshe 87:8, on Tur 87](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/טור_יורה_דעה_פז).

Thus “sixty against the forbidden קיבה” means against **the prohibited stomach milk used**, not merely the stomach skin or an estimated trace of absorbed meat. The mixed-cause explanation assumes a permitted coagulant actually participates; it should not be silently extended to a case containing only forbidden coagulant. Compare **Rema 87:11**, which explicitly permits a combination of permitted and prohibited coagulants as זה וזה גורם, provided there is sixty against the prohibited one. [Shulchan Arukh/Rema 87:11](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שולחן_ערוך_יורה_דעה_פז_יא).
