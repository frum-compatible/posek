# Proposed comparison with practicing rabbonim

**Status: proposed study. No rabbonim have participated, and no human comparison
results exist.** The [current pilot](RESULTS.md) found that Posek and the no-skill
assistant both passed nine selected, AI-reviewed cases. It establishes no
advantage over either an assistant or a rav.

## The question to test

Compare written answers to a defined set of simulated shailos in brachos and
tefillah. Test whether the respondent reaches a defensible conclusion for the
stated facts and minhag, checks the relevant sources, and recognizes when the
facts do not support a ruling. Keep divrei Torah on a separate editorial rubric.

This scope excludes the ongoing relationship, communal authority, and knowledge
of a particular person that a practicing rav brings to an actual shailah.
Results would describe performance on these written tasks. They would not
establish who can serve as a Moirah D'Asrah.

## Participants and questions

Recruit several consenting rabbonim who hold semichah, currently answer practical
shailos, and can document experience in the tested areas. Record years of
practice, relevant training, and the communities and minhagim they serve. A
convenience sample cannot stand for all rabbonim. Publish qualifications and
recruitment criteria with the participants' consent; use participant codes for
answers unless they explicitly agree to attribution.

An independent group of qualified rabbonim should author and review new cases.
Question authors must not be answer participants. Keep those cases and their
grading notes out of skill development and candidate sessions. The public
24-case development suite is already exposed and cannot be the held-out test.

Include ordinary questions, missing controlling facts, mistaken premises,
translation traps, and paired cases where one fact changes the din. Specify the
relevant minhag or make identifying it part of the task. Reviewers should document
accepted positions and their sources before collecting answers. A legitimate
machlokes cannot become an error merely because one reviewer follows another
posek. Preserve disputed labels and explain any later correction.

Use a separate pilot to choose the question count, participant count, and time
budget. Freeze those decisions before the main study. The project has not yet
chosen those numbers or established that recruitment is feasible.

## Three conditions

| Condition | Respondent |
| --- | --- |
| Posek | A recorded model/version with the frozen skill and its documented tools. |
| No-skill assistant | The same model/version and settings, without Posek; the same source-access tools. |
| Human | Each participating rav, using the agreed source-access rules. |

Give every condition the same case facts, requested answer format, clarification
opportunities, and per-case time limit. Supply the same source library and search
access where possible; record any differences, including a human's print sefarim.
Exclude AI assistance from the human condition. A separately labeled study could
later test a rav working with Posek.

Use fresh AI sessions for each case and repeat runs according to a schedule fixed
in advance. Randomize case order and separate paired variants. Record exact
prompts, skill hashes, model identifiers, generation settings, tool permissions,
retrieval failures, elapsed time, and all retries. Save original answers and
tool logs. Keep every scheduled attempt, including failures and refusals.

## Grading and analysis

Have at least two independent, qualified human reviewers grade each answer before
discussing it. Reviewers should have practical expertise in the case's subject and
the relevant minhag. Remove respondent labels and randomize answer order without
rewriting the answers. Voice and formatting may still reveal the condition;
record this limit rather than claiming perfect blinding.

Use the [development rubric](README.md#rubric) as a draft for reviewers to revise
and freeze before the study. Score conclusion, source fidelity, attribution,
minhag and scope, uncertainty, and usefulness. Record disagreements, individual
scores, and the reason for each adjudication. AI grading may assist an audit but
cannot replace this human review.

Count critical errors separately: invented decisive sources, materially wrong
practical conclusions, concealment of failed verification, and inappropriate
certainty where decisive facts are missing. A high prose score must not cancel
one of these. Report abstentions and unnecessary referrals as well as correct
answers. Assess whether expressed confidence matches the support for each
conclusion; fluent certainty earns no credit by itself.

Before collecting the main answers, publish the primary outcome, exclusions,
planned comparisons, and analysis method. Report uncertainty around differences
and account for repeated answers by the same participant, related cases, and
repeated model runs. Have a statistician review the design if the intended claim
is superiority or noninferiority. An “at least as good” claim needs a justified
noninferiority margin and a study designed to assess it. A tie or inconclusive
comparison does not establish equivalence; see the [methodological explanation
of equivalence testing](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177).

## Publication requirements

Release the protocol, case sources, unchanged answers where consent permits,
reviewer qualifications, scores, disagreements, exclusions, and deviations.
Publish the complete results even if the human condition wins. Apply agreed
redactions before releasing any participant material.

Any performance headline must name the task, participating group, model version,
tools, sample sizes, and uncertainty. “Better than a rabbi” would erase those
conditions. Fabricated participants, ratings, or results have no place in this
record.
