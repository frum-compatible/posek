## DT-001 — small vessels

The argument connects the Gemara’s stated reason—refusal to steal—with care for possessions and money owed to others. The final debt example is specific and follows the argument. The gift to Esav distinguishes purposeful expenditure from loss; the adjacent night warning prevents glorifying danger.

The episode and attributions agree with [Chullin 91a:22–23](https://www.sefaria.org/Chullin.91a.22-23), [Rashi on Chullin 91a:22:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Chullin.91a.22.1), [Rashi on 91a:23:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Chullin.91a.23.2), and [Rashi on Bereishis 32:25:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Genesis.32.25.1). The gift’s scale, purpose, and acceptance are supported by [32:14–16](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.32.14-16), [32:21](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.32.21), and [33:11](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.33.11). The economic explanation is a plausible suggested reading, not an established exclusive interpretation.

**Editorial reservation:** The separate danger paragraph interrupts the route to the debt example. Compressing that qualification could improve the spoken arc while preserving its limit. The register fits the audience, but the number of source turns makes three minutes dense.

## DT-002 — service, reward, and education

The distinction between reward as consequence and reward as motive is precise. [Avos 1:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.1.3) and [Teshuvah 10:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Repentance.10.2) supply the central relationship; [10:5](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Repentance.10.5) adds love of the Commander and gradual education. The answer preserves the ideal’s high level and the legitimacy of continuing with imperfect motives. Its notes acknowledge that [10:4](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Repentance.10.4) explicitly supplies the servant reading.

**Editorial reservation:** The pedagogical question creates a second arc after the motive question. A tighter transition would improve oral retention. The extra line of ameilus is correctly distinguished from a prescribed Rambam practice. This piece suits an audience comfortable with its learning vocabulary; linking these sources does not itself establish a new chiddush.

## DT-006 — wedding speech and unverified attribution

The preface clearly declines to authenticate the saying or invent a sefer and page. Its historical attribution remains unresolved. [Bereishis 18:12–14](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.18.12-14) and the school of Rabbi Yishmael’s teaching in [Bava Metzia 87a:10–11](https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Metzia.87a.10-11) support the source account.

The interpretive step notices that the substantive question remains while the hurtful remark is omitted. The application is marked as a suggestion and explicitly preserves necessary, honest conversation; it does not advise suppressing legitimate concerns.

**Editorial reservations:** The spoken text opens with the supplied maxim in quotation marks, while its provenance disclaimer remains outside delivery. Introducing it orally as a thought, or opening with the source, would reduce any remaining aura of a received quotation. The waiting-and-worry example works but is somewhat admonitory for a wedding; a warmer example could serve the same distinction.

## DT-009 — revision for teenagers

The revision explicitly removes the unverified Chazal attribution. The contrast between Avraham’s small offer and larger action, and its attribution to Rabbi Elazar, agrees with [Bereishis 18:2–8](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.18.2-8) and [Bava Metzia 87a:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Metzia.87a.2). The school example is accessible and gives the kindness theme concrete substance without decorative terminology.

**Editorial reservations:** The question about why the Gemara notices Avraham’s words is a teaching hook, not a difficult textual contradiction. The last paragraph partly repeats what the school example already shows; shortening it would allow slower delivery. Its familiar idea is handled well without a novelty claim.
