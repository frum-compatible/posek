**For delivery — approximately two minutes:**

“A Jewish home is built through the words each spouse chooses not to say.”

That thought needs a careful definition. Which words should remain unsaid?

In Parshas Vayeira, Sarah speaks about her own age and adds, “Va’adoni zaken”—my husband is old. When HaKadosh Baruch Hu reports her reaction to Avraham, He says, “Va’ani zakanti”—I have grown old. Her remark about Avraham is left out.

The Gemara in Bava Metzia, daf pei-zayin amud aleph, explains: “Gadol shalom.” So great is shalom that HaKadosh Baruch Hu Himself changed the wording for its sake.

But look carefully at what remains in the conversation. Hashem still asks why Sarah laughed. And He still asks whether anything is beyond His power. The issue is addressed. The words about Avraham’s age are omitted.

Perhaps we can take from this a very practical avodah for a chosson and kallah. A concern may need to be expressed. Yet we can examine the sentence before saying it: Which words explain what is troubling me? Which words only make the other person feel small?

“I was waiting, and I was worried” gives the other person something to understand. Adding “You never think about anyone else” turns that concern into an attack. Leaving those extra words unsaid takes gevurah. It also makes room for the conversation that actually needs to happen.

May the chosson and kallah be zocheh to a bayis filled with words of Torah, appreciation, and honest conversation; and may each have the sensitivity to leave out the needless hurt. That, too, is part of building their bayis ne’eman b’Yisrael.
